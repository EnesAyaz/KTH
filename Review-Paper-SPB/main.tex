\title{Stacked Polyphase Bridge Converters for High-Voltage Traction Drives:
A Review and System-Level Design Perspectives}

{Ayaz \textit{et al.}}

\begin{abstract}
Increasing dc-link voltages in electric traction drives intensify semiconductor voltage stress and insulation requirements. Stacked polyphase bridge (SPB) converters partition the dc-link voltage among series-connected three-phase bridges, each supplying an electrically separated machine winding group. This architecture enables lower-voltage devices and modular converter--machine integration while introducing coupled control and design challenges. This paper reviews SPB topology evolution, operating principles, machine integration, modulation, dc-link balancing, semiconductor scaling, and fault-tolerant reconfiguration. The review synthesizes carrier-interleaving effects on capacitor ripple, conditions and limitations of common-mode cancellation, and balancing requirements under different control structures. It relates cell count and device voltage class to winding, insulation, gate-drive, cooling, and fault-management requirements. These relationships are consolidated into an iterative device-to-system design framework, supported by an illustrative high-voltage traction example. The synthesis indicates that semiconductor-level gains alone do not establish a system-level advantage and that the preferred cell count depends on application requirements and operating conditions. Remaining research priorities include joint battery--converter--machine design and validation against mission energy, volume, cost, and reliability. The review provides a basis for assessing the practical suitability of SPB architectures for high-voltage traction drives.
\end{abstract}
