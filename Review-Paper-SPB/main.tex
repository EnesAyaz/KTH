\title{Stacked Polyphase Bridge Converters for High-Voltage Traction Drives:
A Review and System-Level Design Perspectives}

\author{ENES AYAZ\affilmark{1}, STUDENT MEMBER, IEEE,
SHAHRIAR SARMAST GHAHFAROKHI\affilmark{1}, STUDENT MEMBER, IEEE,
KEVIN KLARIN \affilmark{1}, STUDENT MEMBER, IEEE, AND

\affil{Division of Electric Power and Energy Systems,
KTH Royal Institute of Technology, SE-100 44 Stockholm, Sweden}
\corresp{CORRESPONDING AUTHOR: Enes Ayaz (e-mail: enesa@kth.se).}
\markboth{Stacked Polyphase Bridge Converters for High-Voltage Electric Traction Drives}
{Ayaz \textit{et al.}}

\begin{abstract}
Stacked polyphase bridge (SPB) converters partition a high dc-link voltage among series-connected three-phase bridges, each supplying an electrically separated machine winding group. 
This architecture enables lower-voltage semiconductor devices and modular converter--machine integration, but requires coordinated modulation, voltage balancing, and fault management. 
This paper reviews SPB topology evolution, operating principles, machine integration, semiconductor scaling, and control strategies for high-voltage traction drives. 
It synthesizes the effects of carrier interleaving on dc-link ripple, the conditions and limitations of common-mode cancellation, and the balancing behavior of different control structures.
 The review connects cell count and device voltage class with winding configuration, gate-drive complexity, cooling, and post-fault operation. 
 These interdependencies inform an iterative system-design framework, illustrated by a high-voltage traction example. 
 The synthesis shows that semiconductor-level benefits must be evaluated alongside the additional converter and machine requirements, 
 and that the preferred cell count depends on the application and operating mission. Joint battery--converter--machine design 
are identified as priorities for establishing the practical competitiveness of SPB traction drives.
\end{abstract}
