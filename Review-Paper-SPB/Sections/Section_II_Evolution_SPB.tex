\section{Evolution of the Stacked Polyphase Bridge Concept}
\label{sec:evolution}
% ============================================================================


SPB emerged from a broader development toward modular
converter--machine systems. Earlier modular and integrated modular
motor drives (IMMDs) established building blocks that SPB later drew
on -- repeated converter-winding modules, machine segmentation, power
sharing, and fault-tolerant operation \cite{Cengelci2000MMMI,
Choi2011IMMD} -- and series-connected IMMD modules showed that the
total dc voltage could be shared among inverter segments to access
lower-voltage devices \cite{Wang2013GaNIMMD,Wang2025GaNIMMD,
Wang2015GaNIMMD}. Independently, other series-connected modular
motor-drive topologies were proposed on the same dc-voltage-sharing
principle \cite{Wang2015SeriesModular,Ruseler2015InputSeries}, and a
KTH comparative study benchmarked SPB against other modular-converter
candidates such as the parallel-connected polyphase bridges (PPBs) on
machine design, losses, capacitor energy, cost, and redundancy
\cite{Zhang2017ModularDriveComparison}, confirming SPB as one of
several promising topology candidates.

The SPB topology itself was introduced in 2013 by Norrga \textit{et al.}  \cite{Norrga2013SPB},
followed by dedicated control and modulation work
\cite{Jin2014Control}. 
Related series-connected drives showed that
machine-side degrees of freedom could regulate internal dc-link
sharing \cite{Che2014SeriesConnected}, while Nikouie \textit{et al.}
formally established the dc-link stability problem and its
balancing-controller requirements \cite{Nikouie2015Stability,
Nikouie2017Stability}, which identified dc-link balancing, rather than
semiconductor voltage rating alone, as SPB's first-order control
challenge.

Development then shifted toward modulation, scalable control, and
converter--machine co-design: modulation was shown to shape switching
loss and dc-link harmonics \cite{Jin2017Modulation}, distributed
control offered an alternative to centralized balancing
\cite{Jin2018Distributed}, and interleaving and phase-count studies
tied converter design to machine architecture
\cite{Verkroost2019Interleaving,VanDamme2019DCLink,Bringezu2020SPB,
Verkroost2021Ripple} -- reframing SPB from a converter topology into a
joint converter--machine design problem.

Most recently, work has moved from statically configured hardware
toward actively reconfigurable systems: common-duty-ratio operation,
decentralized balancing, and multi-agent power distribution have
reduced reliance on centralized control \cite{Mocevic2023CDR,
Verkroost2024VoltageBalance,Verkroost2024PowerDistribution,
Mocevic2024GateDelay}, and online module isolation and reintroduction
have demonstrated dynamic dc-bus reconfiguration that removes a
faulted cell without interrupting torque production
\cite{Verkroost2024Reconfiguration}. Modern GaN devices have in
parallel renewed interest in SPB as a route to lower semiconductor
voltage classes \cite{Rohner2024GaN}.

milestones on a chronological timeline, together with 
the implication each result carries for the
system-level review developed in this paper.
