\section{Modulation Strategies}
\label{sec:modulation}
% ============================================================================

Each stacked polyphase bridge (SPB) cell supports conventional sinusoidal
pulsewidth modulation (SPWM), space-vector modulation (SVM), and
discontinuous pulsewidth modulation (DPWM). However, the shared dc
current, dynamic cell voltages, and electromagnetic coupling between
winding groups require coordination across cells. Carrier phase,
zero-sequence injection, switching sequence, and clamping intervals
provide additional degrees of freedom for shaping dc-link ripple,
machine harmonics, switching loss, common-mode voltage (CMV), and
winding-group power distribution \cite{Jin2014Control,Jin2017Modulation}.
Table~\ref{fig:spb_modulation_taxonomy} groups these degrees of freedom
by their principal system-level effects.

\subsection{Carrier Coordination and Harmonic Cancellation}

The switching function of phase leg $x$ in cell $k$ has the
double-Fourier expansion
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
s_{x,k}(t)={}&\frac{A_{00}}{2}
+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big[A_{0n}\cos(n[\omega_{0,k}t+\theta_{0,k}])
+B_{0n}\sin(\cdot)\big]\\
&+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\big[A_{m0}\cos(m[\omega_{c,k}t+\theta_{c,k}])
+B_{m0}\sin(\cdot)\big]\\
&+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\substack{n=-\infty\\n\neq0}}^{\infty}
\Big[A_{mn}\cos\big(m[\omega_{c,k}t+\theta_{c,k}]\\
&\hspace{4.7em}+n[\omega_{0,k}t+\theta_{0,k}]\big)
+B_{mn}\sin(\cdot)\Big],
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:dfs_switching}
\end{equation}
where $m$ and $n$ denote the carrier and sideband indices,
respectively; $\omega_{c,k}$ and $\theta_{c,k}$ are the carrier
frequency and phase; and $\omega_{0,k}$ and $\theta_{0,k}$ are the
fundamental frequency and phase. Each sine term has the same argument
as its corresponding cosine term.

The carrier phase is a free parameter for shaping aggregate harmonics.
Uniform interleaving assigns
\begin{equation}
\theta_{c,k}=\theta_{c,1}+(k-1)\Delta\theta_c,
\qquad
\Delta\theta_c=\frac{2\pi}{N}.
\label{eq:carrier_phase}
\end{equation}
Fig.~\ref{fig:carrier_sync_mechanism} illustrates this arrangement for
three cells.

\subsection{DC-Link Ripple and Battery-Side Current}

DC-link current ripple is a design concern because of its influence
on battery aging. Lower-voltage switches typically permit higher
switching frequencies within a given loss budget, reducing per-cell
capacitance requirements and battery-side ripple relative to a single
high-voltage bridge. Carrier interleaving provides further ripple
reduction.

The dc-side current spectrum is obtained by convolving each phase
switching-function spectrum with its current spectrum,
\begin{equation}
I_{\mathrm{conv},x,k}=\tfrac12\left(S_{x,k}\otimes I_{x,k}\right).
\label{eq:conv_current_spectrum}
\end{equation}
and summing the three phase contributions. For a single cell, carrier
harmonic $m$ produces sidebands at $n=\pm6p-3$ for odd $m$ and
$n=\pm6p$ for even $m$, where $p$ is an integer. With uniform
interleaving, $\theta_{c,k}=2\pi k/N$, the summed converter currents
retain carrier orders $m=pN$ for even $N$ and $m=2pN$ for odd $N$,
with the corresponding sideband structure \cite{Jin2014Control}.

The shared dc-link current follows from the equivalent circuit
developed in Section~\ref{sec:dclink_balancing}:
\begin{equation}
L\frac{di_{\mathrm{dc}}}{dt}=E-Ri_{\mathrm{dc}}-\sum_kv_{\mathrm{conv},k},
\qquad
C_k\frac{dv_{\mathrm{conv},k}}{dt}=i_{\mathrm{dc}}-i_{\mathrm{conv},k},
\label{eq:dclink_ode}
\end{equation}
For equal cell capacitances, $C_k=C$, the relationship between the
summed converter-current spectrum and dc-bus current is
\cite{Jin2014Control}
\begin{equation}
I_{\mathrm{dc}}(\omega)=\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{N}I_{\mathrm{conv},k}(\omega)}
{N-\omega^2LC+j\omega CR}.
\label{eq:battery_current_tf}
\end{equation}
% Editorial note: verify the odd-N surviving carrier order against the
% m=N approximation below before finalizing the technical analysis.
Approximating the residual ripple by the lowest surviving carrier
harmonic, $m=N$, gives
\begin{equation}
\Delta i_{\mathrm{dc}}\approx\frac{\left|\sum_{k=1}^{N}
I_{\mathrm{conv},k}(2\pi Nf_{sw})\right|}
{\left|N-(2\pi Nf_{sw})^2LC+j2\pi Nf_{sw}CR\right|}.
\label{eq:dclink_ripple_estimate}
\end{equation}
Interleaving therefore combines cancellation of lower-order components
with greater $LC$ attenuation at the retained frequency, $Nf_{sw}$.
Fig.~\ref{fig:dclink_ripple_interleaving} illustrates the resulting
reduction for $N=3$.

The simulation includes lumped battery-to-stack inductance but assumes
ideal cell connections. In distributed implementations, bus-bar and
connector-loop inductances alter harmonic cancellation. A
series-connected gallium nitride integrated modular motor drive
required careful capacitor selection and compact layout to preserve
interleaving benefits \cite{Wang2015GaNIMMD}; an earlier
characterization found that parasitic loop inductance could make
interleaved ripple exceed the noninterleaved value
\cite{Wang2015ThesisDVB}. The illustrated reduction should therefore
be treated as an upper bound: achievable performance also depends on
layout, capacitor selection, and switching frequency.

\subsection{Machine Harmonics, Voltage Utilization, and Switching Loss}

The preferred carrier displacement depends on cell count, modulation
index, power factor, winding displacement, and the optimization
objective. SPB studies demonstrate its influence on semiconductor
losses and dc-link current harmonics \cite{Jin2017Modulation}, while
related modular and multiphase drives demonstrate simultaneous
reductions in dc-link and machine current ripple
\cite{Verkroost2021Ripple,DeMarcos2025InterleavedPWM,Wang2025HybridPWM}.

In multiphase machines, nonfundamental voltages can excite low-impedance
harmonic subspaces, increasing copper loss and torque ripple despite
satisfactory fundamental current control. Interleaved and discontinuous
modulation strategies therefore require adaptation to the multiphase
current-ripple characteristics
\cite{Hopkins2019DCLinkRipple,Mohamed2020DCLinkSizing,Alosa2019ModularRipple}.

At high speed, voltage utilization becomes critical because each
winding group is supplied by its local cell voltage $V_{\mathrm{dc},k}$,
subject to the machine voltage limit in
\eqref{eq:spb_machine_voltage_limit}. This constraint becomes more
restrictive as cell count increases and local voltage decreases.
Optimized vector selection in dual-three-phase traction machines can
extend operation in the high-modulation region while suppressing
unwanted subspace currents \cite{Chen2026HighModulationDTP}.

Switching-loss reduction introduces a competing objective. DPWM and
hybrid strategies reduce transitions by clamping selected phases or
redistributing high-frequency switching among modules. Related
open-end-winding studies demonstrate simultaneous reductions in
switching loss and zero-sequence current
\cite{Yang2026HybridOEW,Lee2026AlternatingDPWM}. Although their dc
architecture differs, the principle of coordinating switching activity
among inverter modules is applicable to SPB.

\subsection{Coupling to Voltage Balancing and Common-Mode Control}

Modulation also determines the control authority available for dc-link
voltage balancing and CMV reduction. Voltage balancing requires
differential winding-group power commands that remain feasible within
local voltage and current limits
\cite{Verkroost2024PowerDistribution,Wu2020ActiveBalancing,Mocevic2024GateDelay}.
Redundant voltage vectors and coordinated zero-sequence injection can
reduce common-mode excitation of the machine frame
\cite{Sun2025CMVMPC,Zheng2025MultiObjectivePWM,Chen2020EMCIntegrated}.
These objectives are examined in
Sections~\ref{sec:dclink_balancing} and~\ref{sec:common_mode}, respectively.

SPB modulation consequently requires coordinated switching decisions
that account for ripple, losses, voltage utilization, balancing, and
common-mode behavior across the operating map.
