\section{DC-Link Dynamics, Stability, and Voltage Balancing}
\label{sec:dclink_balancing}
% ============================================================================

The common dc current and independently controlled winding-group
powers (Section~\ref{sec:operating_principle}) couple the local capacitor
energies, so equal cell voltages are not inherently maintained
\cite{Nikouie2015Stability,Nikouie2017Stability}. Related series-connected
motor-drive research likewise shows that distributed current control
alone does not ensure stable voltage sharing \cite{Wang2015ThesisDVB}.

For cell $k$, the power balance is
\begin{equation}
C_kV_{\mathrm{dc},k}\frac{dV_{\mathrm{dc},k}}{dt}
=
V_{\mathrm{dc},k}i_{\mathrm{dc}}
-p_{\mathrm{ac},k}
-p_{\mathrm{loss},k}.
\label{eq:spb_voltage_dynamic}
\end{equation}
Because the series current is common, dc input power depends on local
voltage, whereas the winding-group controller determines ac output
power. Defining the deviation from the mean cell voltage as
\begin{equation}
\widetilde V_k=
V_{\mathrm{dc},k}
-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}V_{\mathrm{dc},j}.
\label{eq:cell_voltage_deviation}
\end{equation}
a simplified differential-mode model around a balanced operating point is
\begin{equation}
CV_0\frac{d\widetilde V_k}{dt}
\approx
I_{\mathrm{dc},0}\widetilde V_k-\Delta P_k.
\label{eq:linearized_balance_dynamic}
\end{equation}
where $C$ and $V_0$ are the common cell capacitance and equilibrium
voltage, $I_{\mathrm{dc},0}$ is the equilibrium dc current, and
$\Delta P_k$ is the differential output-power perturbation. During
motoring, $I_{\mathrm{dc},0}>0$, a positive voltage perturbation increases
cell input power and reinforces the deviation if output power remains
unchanged. During regeneration, this feedback reverses. This
interpretation agrees with SPB stability analyses
\cite{Nikouie2015Stability,Nikouie2017Stability} and related
series-connected multiphase-drive studies \cite{Ni2025SeriesDCLinkStability}.

The constant-power-load model considered in \cite{Nikouie2017Stability}
comprises
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
L_b\frac{di_{\mathrm{dc}}}{dt}
&=E_b-R_bi_{\mathrm{dc}}-\sum_kV_{\mathrm{dc},k},\\
C_k\frac{dV_{\mathrm{dc},k}}{dt}
&=i_{\mathrm{dc}}-\frac{P_k}{V_{\mathrm{dc},k}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:spb_constant_power_model}
\end{equation}
Here, $E_b$ is the dc-source voltage, and $L_b$ and $R_b$ are the
lumped series inductance and resistance of the shared source-to-stack
supply path, including source and interconnection contributions;
$P_k$ is the cell's constant power demand. Under this model's
assumptions, linearization about the open-loop motoring equilibrium
for $N>1$ yields $N-1$ strictly positive real eigenvalues, establishing
instability irrespective of parameter selection.

Fig.~\ref{fig:spb_balancing_dynamics} illustrates the nonlinear dynamics
using $L_b=8\,\mu\mathrm{H}$, $R_b=0.35\,\Omega$, and
$C=100\,\mu\mathrm{F}$ per cell, taken from \cite{Nikouie2017Stability}.
Without balancing, the initial mismatch grows until one cell approaches
voltage collapse. A proportional differential-power law,
$\Delta P_k=K_p\widetilde V_k$, motivated by controller alternative I
in \cite{Nikouie2017Stability}, suppresses the imbalance on a comparable
timescale. This simulation assumes ideal power-command tracking;
it does not reproduce the complete current-control implementation.

The winding groups provide control authority over internal capacitor
energy through differential power commands:
\begin{equation}
P_k^\star=\frac{P^\star}{N}+\Delta P_k,
\qquad
\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Delta P_k=0,
\label{eq:balancing_power}
\end{equation}
This allocation redistributes cell power without changing the total
machine-power reference. Available balancing authority depends on
phase-current margin, winding voltage, torque-ripple limits, and
thermal state.

Table~\ref{fig:spb_balancing_taxonomy} summarizes three principal
approaches. Centralized control explicitly regulates voltage sharing
through differential commands \cite{Nikouie2015Stability,Nikouie2017Stability}.
Common-duty-ratio (CDR) control instead exploits the coupled
converter--machine dynamics; stable sharing has been demonstrated for
the investigated induction-machine system \cite{Mocevic2023CDR}, with
practical performance sensitive to timing mismatch \cite{Mocevic2024GateDelay}.
Distributed multi-agent control extends local coordination to voltage
balancing, power sharing, and reconfiguration
\cite{Verkroost2024VoltageBalance,Verkroost2024PowerDistribution,
Verkroost2024Reconfiguration}. Related modular-drive work uses local
speed-droop laws to coordinate module power sharing
\cite{Galassini2016ModularSpeedDroop}.

For automotive applications, balancing must remain effective during
torque transients, field weakening, parameter mismatch, and module
reconfiguration. Sensor offsets, communication latency, propagation
delays, and differential power limits determine the usable cell-voltage
margin and therefore influence semiconductor voltage-rating selection.
Dc-link stability must consequently be assessed across the coupled
converter, machine, and control system.
