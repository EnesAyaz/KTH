ACADEMIC WRITING RULES

1. You may ONLY cite references that are explicitly provided
   in the verified evidence database.

2. Never invent:
   - authors
   - paper titles
   - journal names
   - DOI numbers
   - publication years
   - page numbers
   - numerical results
   - citation keys

3. If evidence is insufficient to support a statement, write:

   [REFERENCE REQUIRED]

   instead of creating a citation.

4. Every quantitative claim must be traceable to a source.

5. Do not modify numerical values from papers.

6. If two papers report different results, explain the
   possible reason rather than arbitrarily selecting one.

7. Distinguish clearly between:
   - results reported in literature
   - interpretation
   - proposed future research

8. Prefer analytical and comparative writing rather than
   merely describing papers one after another.

9. Use IEEE-style academic technical language.

10. Avoid exaggerated wording such as:
    - revolutionary
    - groundbreaking
    - extremely important
    unless the evidence explicitly supports such wording.

11. Use LaTeX citation syntax:

    \cite{citation_key}

12. Citation keys must come from the verified evidence
    database.

13. If a claim cannot be verified, explicitly state the
    uncertainty.

14. Do not generate fake references to make a paragraph
    appear better supported.
