Design and Evaluation of a 650 V , 300 A GaN-
Based Power Module with Integrated Drivers and 
Ultra-Low Inductance Layout 
Mohammad Dehan Rahman, Tanzila Akter, Abu Shahir Md Khalid Hasan, Hamood-Ur- Rehman, Yuxiang Chen, 
H. Alan Mantooth and Xiaoqing Song 
 Department of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science Engineering, University of Arkansas, Fayetteville, 
Arkansas/United States, mr117@uark.edu  
Abstract— Gallium Nitride (GaN) devices offer high 
switching speed, low loss, and compact design. To fully exploit 
these advantages, packaging is critical. In this paper, a 650 V/ 
300 A half bridge GaN power module is designed and packaged. 
The module features a multilayer substrate design, embedded 
decoupling capacitors, and co-packaged gate drivers, enabling a 
fast switching speed with reduced parasitic inductances. A low 
power loop inductance with about 40% reduction (~5.52 nH) in 
the proposed design is achieved compared conventional single-
layer substrate design (9.1 nH). Thermal performance is 
analyzed with finite element analysis (FEA) to evaluate the high 
current operation. Experimental results validate the module 
concept with fast switching and reduced switching losses. The 
proposed design demonstrates a path toward reliable high-
current GaN modules for electric vehicle chargers and 
industrial systems. 
Keywords— Gallium Nitride (GaN), multilayer substrate, 
Q3D, high current operation, finite element. 
I. INTRODUCTION  
Gallium Nitride (GaN) HEMT technology has seen rapid 
progress in recent years. Manufacturing costs have steadily 
decreased, making these devices increasingly accessible. 
Today, 650 V GaN devices are becoming strong alternatives 
to conventional silicon MOSFETs in low- and medium-
voltage power conversion applications. Compared to silicon, 
GaN devices offer higher switching speeds, lower switching 
losses, and greater power density. These characteristics allow 
for more compact and efficient designs. As a result, GaN 
devices are being widely adopted in data centers, on-board 
chargers (OBCs), renewable energy systems, and motor drive 
applications. Their fast switching capability also enables 
improved system dynamics and reduced cooling 
requirements, supporting the trend toward more energy-
efficient power electronics. 
To fully utilize the advantages of GaN devices, the 
packaging of high-current GaN power modules has become a 
critical research area in both academia and industry [5]. 
Leading manufacturers, including Infineon, GaN Systems, 
Transphorm, and EPC, are actively developing advanced 
module platforms. These designs often combine multiple GaN 
dies in parallel to achieve current ratings that exceed 150 A 
[6]–[8]. Such parallelization is essential for meeting the 
demands of high-power applications while maintaining 
compact form factors. 
Academic groups have also introduced innovative 
approaches. Several research efforts have focused on reducing 
parasitics through the use of embedded substrates, planar 
interconnection schemes, and integrated decoupling 
capacitors. These techniques enable low-inductance half-
bridge configurations, which are crucial for GaN devices 
operating at high switching speeds. The reported modules 
demonstrate fast and stable switching with minimized voltage 
overshoot and reduced ringing [9]- [11]. Beyond performance, 
these designs also highlight the importance of packaging in 
ensuring system reliability, thermal management, and 
electromagnetic compatibility. 
Integration has become an important trend in GaN module 
development. Power modules such as Infineon’s CoolGaN™ 
devices and Navitas’s integrated GaN platforms, combine 
multiple functions into a single package. These include gate 
drivers, protection features, and monitoring circuits. By 
bringing these elements together, these modules can achieve 
faster switching performance, simplified system design, and 
improved reliability. 
Despite these advantages, current efforts have been mostly 
limited to low and medium power applications. Only a few 
studies have focused on high-current GaN modules that can 
operate under the demanding requirements of electric vehicle 
on-board chargers (OBCs), DC fast chargers, and industrial 
power conversion systems [12-20]. Developing such modules 
remains a challenge due to the combined needs of thermal 
management, low-inductance packaging, and system-level 
integration. Addressing these challenges will be critical for 
enabling GaN technology to expand into high-power, high-
current domains. 
This work presents a highly integrated 650 V GaN power 
module rated at 300 A. The design features ultra-low parasitic 
inductance and supports high switching speeds. A multi-layer 
substrate, embedded decoupling capacitors, and co-packaged 
gate drivers are heterogeneously integrated. This approach 
minimizes both power and gate loop inductances, enabling 
reliable fast switching and significantly improving efficiency 
and power density at the system level.

65 mm × 25 mm. Both DC+ and DC− terminals are located 
on the same side, which simplifies system integration and 
reduces layout complexity. The module is designed as a half-
bridge configuration, featuring integrated decoupling 
capacitors and built-in gate drivers. Each switch position

V.  PARASITIC INDUCTANCE SIMULATION  
Minimizing parasitic inductance is one of the prima ry 
goals in power module packaging. For GaN devices, reduced 
inductance directly improves switching performance.  It also 
lowers voltage overshoot, decreases device stress, and helps 
suppress electromagnetic interference (EMI).  
To evaluate the proposed design, a Q3D finite eleme nt 
analysis was carried out using ANSYS. Power loop wa s 
extracted. The analysis was performed at a referenc e 
frequency of 10 MHz, which is commonly used for hig h-
frequency module evaluation. The results show that the power 
loop parasitic inductance is 5.52 nH. This confirms  that the 
proposed architecture provides a significant reduction in loop 
inductance. This is due to the magnetic flux cancel lation, as 
the current loop flows in opposite direction. The current loop 
of the designed module is shown in Fig. 3. 
For comparison, a conventional single-layer design 
exhibits a power loop inductance of 9.1 nH, as summarized in 
Table IV. The reduction achieved demonstrates the 
effectiveness of the multilayer packaging structure . Lower 
inductance not only enables faster switching but also improves 
system efficiency and reliability, especially under high current 
operation.

To evaluate the proposed design, a Q3D finite eleme nt A 
double-pulse test (DPT) was carried out on the fabricated GaN 
module to evaluate its dynamic switching behavior. The 
experimental setup is illustrated in Fig. 6. In thi s

The low-side device was then driven with two consec utive

The test was performed with a DC bus voltage of 350 V. The

measured signals include the gate-to-source voltage  of the

direct insight into the switching dynamics, voltage overshoot,

VII.  C
ONCLUSION  
      A 650 V, 300 A GaN power module has been desi gned 
and demonstrated. The module uses a multilayer subs trate 
with embedded capacitors and co-packaged gate drivers. This 
approach reduces parasitic inductance to 5.52 nH from 9.1 nH 
which is the power loop inductance of a single layered design. 
Inductance extraction confirms significant reductio n 
compared to a single-layer design. The simulated ma ximum 
junction temperature of the module is 118.63  °C with 225 W 
losses under a heat transfer coefficient of 1000 W/ m²·K.  
Experimental tests validate stable switching and re liable 
operation. The results highlight the potential of t he proposed
