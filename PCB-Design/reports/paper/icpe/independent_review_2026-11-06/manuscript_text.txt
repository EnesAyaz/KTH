Topic 17 — Power Electronics for Transportation Electrification 
Abstract— The stacked polyphase bridge (SPB) partitions the dc -link voltage among series-connected submodules, enabling 
low-voltage gallium nitride (GaN) switches to be used in high -voltage traction drives. Its isolated machine winding sets also 
support continued op eration following submodule bypass, distinguishing it from multilevel inverter topologies that require 
additional redundancy for fault tolerance. Realizing the favorable on -state resistance and switching characteristics of low -
voltage GaN at traction-current levels, however, requires coordinated parasitic-aware design. This paper presents a phase-leg 
building block for a 300-kW, 1200-V SPB traction inverter. A charge-based device comparison motivates the selection of the 
100-V EPC2361, while sing le-cell switching and loss assessments identify four parallel devices per switch position as a 
candidate for a sixteen-submodule configuration. Current-sharing sensitivity guides a symmetric arrangement of device pairs. 
An iterative KiCad –Ansys Q3D –LTspice workflow links the routed prototype, extracted parasitic network and switching 
response. With an estimated local commutation-loop inductance of 0.41 nH, optimization of the gate-drive settings achieves a 
simulated peak drain–source voltage of approximately 90 V while retaining fast switching at the assessed operating point. The 
results connect device -level selection to the physical constraints governing low -voltage GaN utilization in an SPB traction 
building block. Experimental validation of one building block is planned for the full paper.  
Index Terms—Electrification of transportation, Gallium nitride, stacked polyphase bridge, traction inverter. 
I. INTRODUCTION 
High dc-link voltages support  megawatt charging and traction power levels of several hundred kilowatts, but conventional 
two-level inverter switches must block the full dc -link voltage while conducting substantial phase current  [1]. Multilevel 
converters enable the use of devices with lower blocking -voltage ratings, although the additional components introduce more 
potential failure points and do not automatically provide fault tolerance. The stacked polyphase bridge (SPB) connects three-
phase bridges in series on the dc side, with each bridge supplying an isolated winding set of a common machine [ 2], [3]. This 
arrangement combines voltage partitioning with a modular structure that supports fault-tolerant operation. A faulty submodule 
can be bypassed while the remaining submodules and winding sets continue operating at reduced capability.  Fig. 1 shows the 
voltage partitioning and the phase-leg building block considered here. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
Fig. 1. (a) SPB topology and isolated winding sets for poly-phase machine; (b) Three-phase submodule; each symbol represents 
four parallel GaN devices, with the diode indicating reverse conduction. 
 
Voltage partitioning enables each SPB submodule to use a lower semiconductor voltage class, creating an opportunity to 
exploit the favorable on -state resistance and switching characteristics of low -voltage gallium nitride (GaN) devices in high -
voltage traction drives [4]. Increasing the submodule count reduces the voltage across each bridge but leaves the phase-current 
requirement unchanged for fixed total power, dc -link voltage, modulation index , and power factor. Several devices may 
therefore be required in parallel, making current sharing, gate-drive impedance, and commutation paths central to realizing the 
expected device benefits. 
Previous studies address SPB modulation and losses [ 3], dc -link stability [ 5], and comparisons with alternative GaN 
converter systems [ 6]. Research on paralleled GaN stages identifies complementary implementation constraints, including 
commutation inductance, source coupling , and unequal gate impedances [ 7]–[9]. These findings indicate that device -level 
comparisons must be complemented by an assessment of the routed building block: a favorable device figure of merit alone 
does not establish sufficient drain- and gate-voltage margins at traction current. Accordingly, this paper evaluates low-voltage 
GaN utilization by linking device selection, single-cell characterization, and parallel-count selection to a symmetric phase-leg 
implementation. A KiCad–Ansys Q3D–LTspice workflow connects the prototype layout to its parasitic network and switching 
response. The contribution is a quantitative assessment of how the power network and gate-drive settings jointly constrain the 
selection of the number of cells and the design of the SPB building block.  The introduction part will be extended in the final 
paper; the reduced version is given in the digest due to the page limitation.

IV. GATE DRIVE CONFIGURATION AND CELL ARRANGEMENT 
A. Current Sharing Sensitivity 
The preliminary parallel-count choice assumes equal current sharing. Fig. 5(a) compares two parallel devices at 75 V and 120

B. Paired Gate Drive Configuration 
Connecting all four devices to one gate-driver output increases the total gate charge and the current required to establish the 
intended switching transition. The turn -off path must also sink the displacement current coupled through the gate –drain 
capacitance during drain -voltage transients. Excessive impedance in this path can cause a positive gate -voltage disturbance 
and reduce the margin against unintended turn -on. The four-device arrangement is divided into two device pairs per switch 
position. As illustrated in Fig. 5(b), each pair of switching cells is served by one dual -channel 2EDF7275K driver [10]. One 
channel drives two high-side GaNs and the other drives two low-side GaNs. The phase leg uses two drivers, with an individual 
gate resistor for each GaN.

D.  Implications for Low Voltage GaN Utilization 
The prototype board results show that a compact power loop is necessary but does not by itself establish usable switching 
margin. Parallel count, branch symmetry and gate-drive settings determine whether the selected voltage class remains practical. 
The higher gate-inductance case leaves no useful drain -voltage margin for tolerances or operating -point variation. If layout 
and drive changes cannot provide that margin, reducing the submodule voltage feeds back into the SPB series count. The lower 
gate-inductance case provides more headroom at this operating point, without establishing compliance over a complete traction 
operating envelope.

VII.  CONCLUSION 
The SPB voltage partition enables low-voltage GaN to be considered for a high-voltage traction drive, but its utilization 
depends on a realizable high-current building block. The device comparison, single-cell study and parallel-count assessment 
lead to a symmetric four-device candidate. Prototype board extraction and switching simulations then expose the remaining 
joint drain- and gate-voltage constraints. These results connect device-level selection to implementation feasibility; they do 
not establish a final loss optimum or full-drive efficiency. Testing one building block in the full paper will assess the 
simulation predictions and remaining switching margin.
