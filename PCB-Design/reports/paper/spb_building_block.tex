\begin{abstract}
In a stacked polyphase bridge (SPB) converter the battery voltage is shared by series-connected three-phase submodules, so the transistor voltage class is a free design variable. This paper designs the phase-leg building block of a 1200\,V, 300\,kW SPB traction inverter. Since the phase current is independent of the number of submodules, low-voltage GaN transistors are favored, and the turn-off overshoot at a realistic commutation-loop inductance sets 16 submodules of 75\,V with 100\,V devices. Four EPC2361 per switch, driven in two identical pairs by one dual-channel isolated driver each, keep the junction temperature at \TjFour\,$^\circ$C and the peak drain--source voltage below 90\,V. The dc link of a three-phase level is sized for the voltage ripple with the dc-bias derating of class-II MLCCs. The leg is built in a double-pulse-test version with a coaxial shunt per cell and a load-test version without, and three legs share a laminated PCB busbar. Electromagnetic extraction of the routed boards gives a commutation-loop inductance of \LoopLoad\,nH per cell without and \LoopDpt\,nH with the shunt, equal in the four parallel cells within \LoopDptSpread\,\% and hardly coupled. The extracted gate loops are equal for the four paralleled devices of a switch but reach \GatePairHS\,nH. A double-pulse simulation with the complete extracted network confirms equal current sharing and shows that the test board needs a slower turn-on and a $-1.9$\,V off-state to stay at \QsVhDpt\,V.
\end{abstract}

GaN, paralleling, parasitic inductance, stacked polyphase bridge, traction inverter.

%=========================================================================
\section{Introduction}
%=========================================================================
Traction batteries of heavy-duty electric vehicles are moving to 800\,V and above, which pushes two-level inverters to 1200\,V transistors with large voltage steps at the machine terminals. The stacked polyphase bridge (SPB) converter~\cite{norrga2013,jin2014} connects $N$ three-phase bridges in series on the dc side, each feeding one winding set of a multiphase machine. Every submodule blocks only $V_\mathrm{bus}/N$, which allows low-voltage transistors, reduces the voltage step at the windings and the dc-link capacitance~\cite{jin2017,bringezu2023}, and allows a faulty submodule to be bypassed. Control~\cite{jin2014,verkroost2024}, dc-link stability~\cite{nikouie2017tpel}, machine design~\cite{zhang2014,bringezu2020} and integration~\cite{nikouie2017} have been studied, and a GaN-based SPB has been found competitive with a three-level flying-capacitor inverter~\cite{rohner2024}.

The submodule itself, however, has received little attention. At 1200\,V and 300\,kW each leg conducts more than 200\,A$_\mathrm{rms}$ at 75\,V, so several GaN transistors must be paralleled. Paralleled low-voltage GaN stages are limited by turn-off overshoot~\cite{brothers2019}, by uneven dynamic current sharing~\cite{lu2017,tran2025} and by gate disturbances~\cite{musumeci2022}; symmetric layouts with up to eight devices per switch have been demonstrated at 48--120\,V~\cite{wattenberg2023,cooke2026}. In those designs the bus voltage is given. In an SPB it is not, and the submodule voltage, the device count and the layout are coupled through the loop inductance.

This paper derives the SPB building block from the inverter specification and verifies the resulting hardware. The contributions are: 1) a selection of the device voltage class and the submodule voltage that accounts for the commutation-loop inductance; 2) a gate-drive arrangement for four paralleled GaN transistors per switch based on a current-sharing sensitivity analysis; 3) a four-cell leg board, built in a test and an operating version, and its verification by Q3D extraction of the routed copper, including the gate network, and by a double-pulse simulation with the complete extracted parasitic network.

\label{fig:spb}

For $N$ submodules sharing the power equally (Fig.~\ref{fig:spb}a), one leg delivers $P_\mathrm{leg}=M V_m I_\mathrm{ph}\cos\varphi/(2\sqrt2)$, so the inverter power is
\begin{equation}
P=3N P_\mathrm{leg}=\frac{3M\,V_\mathrm{bus}}{2\sqrt2}\,I_\mathrm{ph}\cos\varphi .
\label{eq:p}
\end{equation}
The phase current does not depend on $N$: with $M$\,=\,1.15 and $\cos\varphi$\,=\,0.9, 300\,kW at 1200\,V require \Irms\,A$_\mathrm{rms}$ (\Ipk\,A peak) in every leg of every submodule. The number of submodules therefore only scales the voltage, and with it the number of transistors and drivers. The peak drain--source voltage is limited to 90\,V for 100\,V devices, and the switching frequency is set to 50\,kHz to keep the submodule capacitors small.

%=========================================================================
\section{Device and Submodule Voltage}\label{sec:vclass}
%=========================================================================
Since every submodule carries the full phase current, the conduction loss of the inverter scales with $N I^2R_\mathrm{DS(on)}/n$ for $n$ devices per switch, and the output-charge loss with $N n f_\mathrm{sw}C_\mathrm{oss,tr}V_m^2$. Optimizing $n$ gives the minimum loss
\begin{equation}
P_\mathrm{min}=6\,I\,V_\mathrm{bus}\sqrt{f_\mathrm{sw}\,R_\mathrm{DS(on)}C_\mathrm{oss,tr}},
\label{eq:pmin}
\end{equation}
in which the voltage class cancels to first order. Devices are thus ranked by the time constant $\tau=R_\mathrm{DS(on)}C_\mathrm{oss,tr}$ and, because conduction dominates at this current, by the on-resistance per volt of blocking capability. Among ten GaN transistors of the 100--650\,V classes, the 100\,V parts lead in both (Fig.~\ref{fig:dev}a,b); EPC2361 (0.75\,m$\Omega$, 3\,$\times$\,5\,mm) has the lowest $\tau$ and 7.5\,\textmu$\Omega$/V, three to thirty times below the higher-voltage parts. The price is a larger number of submodules (Fig.~\ref{fig:dev}c), which the modular SPB accommodates. The Kelvin source pad of the EPC2361 keeps the source inductance out of the gate loop (Fig.~\ref{fig:spb}c).

With the turn-on rate of a 2EDF7275K-driven EPC2361 ($R_{g,\mathrm{on}}$\,=\,2\,$\Omega$), \DidtEightyA\,A/ns at 80\,A per device, the admissible submodule voltage is \VmaxEightyA\,V at 0.5\,nH and \VmaxEightyB\,V at 1\,nH (Fig.~\ref{fig:vm}).

\section{Devices per Switch and Gate Drive}\label{sec:npar}
%=========================================================================
\subsection{Number of paralleled devices}
Double-pulse simulations of one EPC2361 per switch (LTspice, 75\,V) bound the current per device. At 120\,A the overshoot reaches 102\,V with 0.5\,nH, and at 80\,A already 93\,V with 1\,nH, so the current per device is limited to about 80\,A at the peak. A larger loop lowers the turn-on energy (21.8 versus 28.3\,\textmu J at 80\,A for 1 and 0.5\,nH), because it takes $L_\mathrm{loop}di/dt$ of the voltage during the current rise, but it raises the overshoot and the turn-off energy.

\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{fig_sharing.pdf}

(a) Drain currents at turn-on: (i) symmetric with Kelvin connections, (ii) gate loops of 2 and 8\,nH, (iii) no Kelvin connection and 0.2\,nH common-source mismatch. (b) Turn-on (solid) and turn-off (hatched) energy of both devices.}

The leg loss over a fundamental period is computed from these energies, with the junction temperature and $R_\mathrm{DS(on)}$ solved together for the thermal path of the load-test hardware: a 50\,mm fan heatsink (0.65\,K/W) on an aluminium spreader and, per device, \Rjh\,K/W from junction to heatsink, dominated by the insulating gap pad (\Rpad\,K/W). Fig.~\ref{fig:npar} shows the result. One device per switch runs away thermally and two reach \TjTwo\,$^\circ$C. Three keep $T_j$ at \TjThree\,$^\circ$C but exceed 90\,V. Four devices dissipate \TotFourA\,W per leg (\CondFour\,W conduction) at \TjFour\,$^\circ$C and 40\,$^\circ$C ambient, with \VpkFourA\,V peak voltage at 0.5\,nH but \VpkFourB\,V at 1\,nH. More devices save less than 3\,W per leg but add drivers. Four devices per switch are chosen, which also form two equal pairs for the gate drive below.

Static sharing is benign because $R_\mathrm{DS(on)}$ rises with temperature; dynamic sharing is not. Fig.~\ref{fig:sharing} and Table~\ref{tab:sharing} show the imbalance of the switching energy for one asymmetry at a time. A 6\,nH longer gate loop delays the second device by about half the current rise time and shifts most of the turn-on energy to the first. Gate-loop and common-source mismatches dominate, while a power-loop mismatch of 0.3\,nH is nearly harmless. The gate paths must therefore be equal by construction. Driving all four devices of a switch from one channel is not an option: the slower gate charging raises the turn-on energy by 31\,\% and the peak sink current exceeds the 8\,A rating. Instead, one 2EDF7275K (4/8\,A, isolated high- and low-side channel, 3\,ns channel mismatch) is placed between two cells that are rotated by 180$^\circ$ about it, and drives both gates through a star with Kelvin returns (Fig.~\ref{fig:spb}b); the point symmetry makes both gate branches identical. Each isolated supply is split by a Zener diode into $+5.1$\,V and a negative off-state voltage. The negative bias absorbs the gate bump of the off device, which the Miller current alone raises by $\Delta v_\mathrm{GS}\approx Q_\mathrm{GD}/C_\mathrm{GS}\approx1.06$\,V, otherwise close to the 0.8\,V minimum threshold. Its value, $-1.9$\,V, follows from the extracted gate network in Section~\ref{sec:valid} and keeps the off-state ringing above the $-4$\,V gate limit.

The three legs of a submodule share one dc bus, so the low-frequency ripple cancels and only the switching-frequency component remains. A switching-function model with space-vector modulation gives 85\,A$_\mathrm{rms}$ capacitor current per level and a charge excursion of 860\,\textmu C per switching period at 50\,kHz. A ripple of 3\,\% (2.25\,V) therefore requires 127\,\textmu F effective capacitance per leg board; the voltage ripple, not the current, sizes the dc link. At 75\,V, 100\,V class-II MLCCs retain only 15--29\,\% of their nominal capacitance. The 22\,\uF{} 2220 part with the highest retention (\CtwoRet\,\%, \CtwoEff\,\uF) is selected, and twenty of them are placed on each leg board (Fig.~\ref{fig:dclink}); the ripple current alone would need only \Nicap{} parts.

\section{Leg Board and Verification}\label{sec:board}
%=========================================================================
The four-layer leg board (2\,oz, 0.1\,mm dielectric between L1 and L2) carries the four cells as two identical pairs, each with its driver in the centre between two cells rotated by 180$^\circ$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:board}). Each commutation loop is vertical: the forward path on L1 returns on the dc$-$ plane on L2 directly below. One dc terminal pair feeds a laminated row across the board that supplies both the capacitor bank and all cells, and the four switch nodes are joined in copper and leave through a single bolted ac terminal. Symmetry required two measures. The interleaved drain and source stripes of the EPC2361 leave no room for a via row, so the low-side source stripes use vias in the pad, and any via removed for clearance is removed together with its mirror image. The driver inputs, with common-mode chokes on the supply and disable pairs but none on the PWM lines (no added delay), keep 4.0\,mm on the outer and 3.0\,mm on the inner layers from all power copper, the IPC-2221B spacing for 1200\,V, so the layout is ready for a reinforced isolated driver; the 2EDF7275K used here provides functional isolation for the 75\,V tests.

A shunt in every low-side source is needed to measure the cell currents but lengthens the loop and blocks the heatsink. The board is therefore built in two versions from the same design. The double-pulse-test (DPT) board carries a 1\,m$\Omega$ coaxial shunt (PMK UFCS) per cell on a shortened land and needs no heatsink. The load-test board connects the low-side sources through blind vias to the dc$-$ plane and carries the heatsink.

The routed boards were verified in Ansys Q3D with the loop evaluated with all four cells switching. To keep the solution time short, each quantity is extracted from the smallest model that contains it: the cell band at 100\,MHz for the loop and the full board at dc for the resistances. Table~\ref{tab:q3d} and Fig.~\ref{fig:loop} summarize the results. The load-test board reaches \LoopLoad\,nH per cell, of which \CuLoad\,nH is copper and \EslLoad\,nH capacitor ESL, and the four cells agree within \LoopLoadSpread\,\%. It thus meets the 0.5\,nH target and gives about \VosLoad\,V peak at 30\,A/ns. On the DPT board the shunt and its connection add 0.24\,nH, which costs overshoot margin (Section~\ref{sec:valid}). Beyond four bypass capacitors per cell the loop hardly decreases (Fig.~\ref{fig:loop}a); the remaining capacitors provide capacitance rather than lower inductance. The cells hardly couple (at most \CellMmax\,pH, $k\leq$\,\CellKmax, between the inner cells), so each cell commutates through its own loop.

A second model adds the gate network of one driver block (DPT board); gate and Kelvin return run on adjacent layers from each gate pad to the output capacitors of the driver. A device driven alone sees \GateHS\,nH (high side) and \GateLS\,nH (low side). The star-to-driver part (\GateCommonHS\ and \GateCommonLS\,nH) is shared, so a device of a pair sees \GatePairHS\ and \GatePairLS\,nH. All four paralleled devices of a switch agree within \GateSym\,\%, and the commutation loops couple at most \GateCoupMax\,pH into a gate loop. The gate loop is thus about ten times the lumped 1.7\,nH, because the driver sits between two cells behind the isolation barrier.

\section{Validation With the Extracted Parasitics}\label{sec:valid}
%=========================================================================
The device-level design of Sections~\ref{sec:vclass} and~\ref{sec:npar} relies on a lumped loop. A routed board adds what this model hides: the four cells reach the capacitor bank and the ac terminal through different copper, couple to each other, and see their bypass capacitors at different distances. To check the design against these effects, the extracted copper was inserted into the double-pulse circuit of Section~\ref{sec:npar}: one inductor per terminal with every mutual inductance as a coupling (29 inductors and 400 couplings for the load-test board), the dc resistance matrix through behavioral sources, eight EPC2361 models, the bypass capacitors with dc-bias capacitance and ESL, the shunts, the bank and two drivers with star gate networks. The load current is switched at the peak phase current.

Fig.~\ref{fig:valid} shows the result. The four cells carry \QsIminLoad--\QsImaxLoad\,A at turn-off and their turn-on energies differ by \QsShareEonLoad\,\% (\QsShareEonDpt\,\% on the DPT board), so the mirrored layout and the per-pair gate drive leave none of the sharing mechanisms of Table~\ref{tab:sharing} active. The low-side turn-off is uncritical (\QsVlLoad\,V). The critical event is the low-side turn-on, at which the high-side device is charged at \QsDidtLoad\,A/ns. Its drain--source voltage peaks at \QsVhLoad\,V on the load-test board, in agreement with the lumped model at the extracted loop (\QsLumVhLoad\,V), and at \QsVhDpt\,V on the DPT board. The energies agree with the lumped model within 10\,\% for turn-on (\QsEonLoad{} versus \QsLumEonLoad\,\textmu J) and within 1\,\textmu J for turn-off. The peak gate bump of the high-side devices, \QsBumpLoad\,V with a 0\,V off-state, exceeds the 0.8\,V minimum threshold and confirms the need for the negative off-state voltage of Section~\ref{sec:npar}.

The simulation therefore validates the lumped design of the power loop but shows that the load-test board operates exactly at the 90\,V limit with $R_\mathrm{g,on}$\,=\,2\,$\Omega$. With 3\,$\Omega$ the turn-on slows to \QsDidtLoadRgThree\,A/ns and the peak drops to \QsVhLoadRgThree\,V. The turn-on energy rises by 20\,\% (\QsEonLoadRgThree\,\textmu J per device), which adds less than 1\,W per leg.

The gate loop is the exception. With the extracted gate network of the DPT board (plus the resistor and diode bodies) and $R_\mathrm{g,on}$\,=\,2.5\,$\Omega$, the turn-on reaches \QsDidtDptRgLow\,A/ns and the high-side peak \QsVhDptRgLow\,V (\QsVhDptLump\,V with the lumped gate loop). The DPT board is therefore driven with 3.5\,$\Omega$ (1.5\,$\Omega$ shared per pair) and $-1.9$\,V: the peak is \QsVhDpt\,V with the shunts in the loop, the turn-on energies of the cells agree within \QsShareEonDpt\,\%, and the held-off gate peaks at \QsBumpDpt\,V, 1.3\,V below the minimum threshold, while the off-state ringing (about $-3.4$\,V) stays inside the $-4$\,V limit.

\section{Conclusion}
%=========================================================================
Because the phase current of an SPB does not depend on the number of submodules, the device and the submodule voltage can be chosen for the lowest loss and the available overshoot margin: for 1200\,V and 300\,kW, 16 submodules of 75\,V with four 100\,V GaN transistors per switch, driven in pairs by one isolated dual-channel driver each. Q3D extraction of the routed board gives \LoopLoad\,nH per cell, equal loops in all four cells and negligible coupling between them ($k\leq$\,\CellKmax). A double-pulse simulation with the full extracted parasitic network confirms the lumped design of the power loop: the cells share current within \QsShareLoad\,\% and the switching energy within \QsShareEonLoad\,\%, and a slightly slower turn-on ($R_\mathrm{g,on}$\,=\,3\,$\Omega$) keeps the peak voltage at \QsVhLoadRgThree\,V. The gate loop of the centre driver, equal for all four paralleled devices but about ten times the assumed value, is the main deviation: the test version with shunts (\LoopDpt\,nH) needs 3.5\,$\Omega$ and $-1.9$\,V to stay at \QsVhDpt\,V. A driver stage next to each pair of devices is planned for the next revision. Double-pulse and continuous tests of a three-phase level are the next step.
