## 7. Top 10 improvements (ranked by impact on the meeting)

(quick)** Backup 26 shows the high-side V_GS reaching -4.9 V (limit -4 V) and a +/-15.3 % turn-on split on version 1 with its one AC bolt. ## 1. Story, flow and timing (slides 1-14)

Order today: title -> A (why HV, SPB, why 100 V GaN) -> B (design, flow, waveforms) -> C (four problems overview, then one slide each) -> D (device requirements) -> thank-you with the four asks. The structure is right: the problem slides 9-12 with Problem / Tried / Ask are the strongest part of the deck and should get the most time.

Where the audience gets lost or time is lost:
- **Asks arrive late.** "Expect" is only one grey sentence on slide 1; the four asks appear first at slide 8 (minute about 14). Add a three-line "what we want from this meeting" box on slide 1 (replace the A-D thumbnail captions, which only repeat section names) so the AEs listen with the asks in mind.
- **Section A is heavy for this audience.** Infineon traction AEs know the 800 V story; slide 2 (radar + calculator + table) and slide 4 (two charts + calculator) each take about 3 minutes if presented as built. Slide 2 should be 1 minute.
- **Slide 7 is the bridge from "our design" to "our problems", but it presents a clean result** (0.7 % spread, -0.50 V bump, 92.9 V) that undercuts Problems 2 and 3 a few minutes later. Say on slide 7 which number becomes which problem: "92.9 V: 3 V over our 90 V limit; -0.50 V bump at -1.9 V off -> Problem 2; 0.7 % spread with an ideal AC take-off -> Problem 3".
- **Redundancy:** slide 8 (overview), the Ask boxes on 9-12, slide 13 and slide 14 state the same asks four times with slightly different numbers. Keep 8 short (one line per problem), keep the Ask boxes on 9-12, make 13 the only full spec table, and let 14 copy the exact Ask wording.

## 4. The asks to Infineon

Test: each ask should be answerable with a part number, a yes/no plus date, or a circuit with values.

1. **Driver.** Now: "a GaN driver with >= 15 A sink and split rails, one channel per switch". Issue: 15 A is the n = 4 value of your own formula at 0.35 ohm pull-down; the slide 9 Ask also demands < 0.2 ohm pull-down (that gives about 22 A) and the slide 9 bullet says 15-20 A. Sharper: "Which isolated EiceDRIVER part drives four EPC2361 in parallel (+5 / -1.7 V) with >= 20 A peak sink, <= 0.2 ohm pull-down, separate source/sink outputs and CMTI >= 200 V/ns? If none, do you recommend 2EDF7275K plus an external buffer, and which buffer?" Keep the "2EDF7275K SPICE model with the output current limit" as a separate one-line ask; today it is hidden in the slide 9 box.
2. **Gate bump.** Now: "your solution at 50+ V/ns: bias, drive circuit or device". Too open; the answer will be an app note. Sharper: "At 50 V/ns with 4 x EPC2361 per switch, which do you recommend, with values: the RC-coupled GaN EiceDRIVER (part, R and C), a Miller clamp, or a CoolGaN with higher V_th,min / larger negative V_GS rating? Do you have measured bump vs dv/dt data for your 100 V parts?"
3. **Paralleling.** Now: "V_th distribution data, binning, packages with a Kelvin pin or several dies". Sharper: "For CoolGaN 100 V / 200 V: what is the within-lot and lot-to-lot V_th sigma; do you offer matched bins; is there a multi-die or half-bridge package with Kelvin source that replaces four discrete parts?" Add your acceptance number (from the slide 11 sweep), e.g. "we need E_on within 1.5 : 1, i.e. V_th spread below X V".
4. **Voltage class.** Now: "a 200 V CoolGaN with a Kelvin source: roadmap, samples, models". Sharper: "Is there a CoolGaN 200 V part (now or roadmap, with date) with R_DS(on) <= 1.5 mOhm typ. and C_oss,tr <= 900 pF at 150 V, Kelvin source, top- or bottom-side cooled? 5. **Joint test.** Only in grey small print on slide 8 ("a joint test of one SPB leg with Infineon parts"). This is the most actionable ask for a collaboration; make it ask 5 on slides 8, 13 and 14: "Parts for one SPB leg so we can measure it against our EPC2361 board on the same double-pulse setup (target date)".
