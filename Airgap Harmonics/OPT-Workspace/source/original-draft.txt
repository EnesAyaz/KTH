Design of a Pole-Changing Wound Field Synchronous Machine With
Bipolar Optical Power Transfer Excitation
Enes Ayaz1,Graduate Member, IEEE, Nail Tosun 2,Graduate Member, IEEE
1Department of Electric Power and Energy Systems, KTH Royal Institute of Technology, Stockholm, Sweden
2 Department of Electrical and Electronic Engineering, Newcastle University, Newcastle, United Kingdom
This paper presents the electromagnetic design and analysis of a pole-changing wound field synchronous machine (PC-WFSM)
supplied by a bipolar optical power transfer (OPT) field-excitation system. The proposed rotor excitation architecture uses three
stationary light sources: two optical channels provide reversible excitation for rotor field-coil groups whose current polarity changes
between pole modes, while a third channel supplies the field-coil groups that retain a fixed polarity. In each bipolar channel,
two rotor-mounted photovoltaic (PV) cell groups are selectively illuminated; the inactive PV group provides a passive return path
through its first cell and an external diode. A unified PV-cell model is used to describe both the illuminated power-generating
regime and the dark forward-biased return regime. Based on this model, a corrected maximum power point (MPP) matching
condition is derived and incorporated into the rotor field-winding design, thereby coupling the OPT parameters to the achievable
rotor magnetomotive force (MMF), field resistance, slot geometry, and stack length. Two-dimensional finite element analysis of a 10
kW, 4-pole/2-pole prototype is used to verify the pole transformation, rotor-MMF reconfiguration, airgap flux distribution, no-load
EMF, torque production, and loss performance. The proposed method provides brushless bipolar field excitation for PC-WFSMs
with stationary-side optical control and without rotor-side active switching or resonant compensation components.
Index Terms—Bipolar field excitation, brushless excitation, electrically excited synchronous machine (EESM), finite element analysis
(FEA), optical power transfer (OPT), photovoltaic cell, pole-changing, wound field synchronous machine (WFSM).
I. INTRODUCTION
W
OUND field synchronous machines (WFSMs) have
attracted renewed interest as rare-earth-free alterna-
tives to permanent-magnet machines in traction and power-
generation applications [1]. Their controllable field excitation
enables wide-speed-range operation, flux weakening, and safe
de-excitation under fault conditions. These advantages are es-
pecially attractive when high-speed operation and controllable
airgap flux are required.
Pole-changing operation provides an additional degree of
freedom by modifying the effective pole number to reshape the
torque–speed characteristic over a wide operating range [2]. In
induction machines, the rotor field naturally follows the stator
pole number; in synchronous machines, however, the rotor
excitation pattern must also be reconfigured. Hence, a PC-
WFSM requires not only a pole-changing stator MMF but also
a rotor field system capable of changing the spatial polarity
of selected field coils. This requirement makes bipolar field
excitation a central design constraint rather than an auxiliary
power-supply feature, as illustrated in Fig. 1.
Several brushless excitation methods have been proposed
to avoid slip rings and brushes, but they offer different
compromises in terms of rotor complexity, controllability,
and polarity reversal. Sub-harmonic self-excitation [3] can
realize brushless operation by inducing current in a rotor
harmonic winding and rectifying it through a diode bridge.
However, this approach requires additional rotor windings
and rectifiers and does not directly provide reversible field
current for pole-changing operation. WPT-based excitation [4]
eliminates mechanical contacts, but it generally requires rotor-
mounted resonant networks, rectifiers, and filtering compo-

nents. For bipolar excitation, primary-side controlled WPT

EESMs [5]; nevertheless, the receiver still includes resonant
magnetic components and switching/rectification circuitry.
OPT-based field excitation provides a different route. In the
recent OPT-WFSG design framework [6], a shaft-mounted PV
cell directly supplies the rotor field winding, reducing rotor-
side passive components and making the approach attractive
for high-speed machines. The limitation is that the conven-
tional PV-to-field-winding connection is intrinsically unipolar.
Therefore, the open design problem addressed in this paper is
how to obtain bipolar field excitation for a PC-WFSM while
preserving the rotor simplicity of OPT and, at the same time,
incorporating the resulting OPT constraint into the machine
electromagnetic design.
In this paper, a bipolar OPT excitation system is proposed
for pole-changing WFSMs. Two stationary light sources pro-
vide bipolar excitation for the field coil groups requiring polar-
ity reversal, while a third independent light source supplies the
field coil groups that remain unipolar. In the bipolar channels,

A. Design Constraints and Target Specifications
The proposed pole-changing WFSM is constrained by both
electromagnetic requirements and OPT excitation characteris-
tics. The field current is limited by the PV-cell MPP current,
and the field winding resistance must satisfy the corrected
matching condition in (6). Therefore, the rotor winding, slot
geometry, and machine dimensions must be co-designed with
the OPT parameters.

IV. FINITEELEMENTVALIDATION
A. FEA Setup
Two-dimensional FEA was carried out in ANSYS Maxwell
2024R1 using the nonlinear B–H characteristics of 35PN230
electrical steel. The simulations were performed for both
pole configurations, corresponding to the excitation modes
described in Section II. No-load and loaded operating points
were evaluated to verify the pole transformation, rotor-MMF
reconfiguration, flux distribution, EMF waveform, torque pro-
duction, and loss performance. Core losses were evaluated
using the material loss model of the lamination, while cop-
per losses were calculated from the winding resistances and
current densities.
B. Airgap Flux Density and No-Load EMF
Fig. 6 shows the airgap flux density distribution for both
pole configurations. The 4-pole mode exhibits the expected
N–S–N–S pattern, whereas the 2-pole mode produces a single
N–S pair, confirming correct pole transformation through field-
current reversal.
Fig. 7 presents the corresponding no-load phase EMF wave-
forms. Both configurations exhibit near-sinusoidal behavior,

C. Rotor MMF and Harmonic Content
Because the proposed excitation system is intended to
reconfigure the rotor pole number, the rotor-MMF distribution
is a key validation result. The field-coil polarities in Table I
should be applied to the FEA model and the resulting rotor
MMF should be decomposed into spatial harmonics for both
pole modes. The desired pole harmonic must dominate in
each mode, while the residual unwanted pole harmonic should
remain sufficiently small to avoid excessive torque ripple and
additional core loss.
D. Torque Production and Torque Ripple
The torque capability of the two pole modes should be
evaluated at the same dc-link voltage and current limit used
for the drive system. The 4-pole mode is expected to provide
higher low-speed torque, whereas the 2-pole mode should
extend the high-speed operating range by reducing the elec-
trical frequency. Therefore, the final validation should include
average torque, torque ripple, and, if available, torque–speed
curves for both modes.
E. Representative Operating Points
To demonstrate the usefulness of pole-changing operation,
two representative operating points are evaluated. The first
corresponds to a low-speed, high-torque condition, where the
4-pole configuration is expected to be preferable. The second
corresponds to a high-speed operating condition, where the
2-pole configuration is expected to reduce electromagnetic
frequency and improve efficiency.

The additional loss introduced by the bipolar OPT excitation

G. Rotor-Side OPT Loss and Thermal Check
The rotor-side excitation loss consists of the field copper
loss, the return-path conduction loss, and the PV-cell con-
version loss. In the proposed bipolar path, the most critical
additional thermal stress is concentrated in the first inactive PV
cell that conducts the return current. Therefore, the final design
should verify that the dark-cell forward current, external diode
current, and local heat generation remain within the allowable
temperature limits under continuous excitation.
V. COMPARISONWITHPRIORMETHODS
Table VI compares the proposed excitation system with
representative brushless WFSM excitation methods in terms
of bipolar capability, rotor-side hardware, pole-changing
suitability, and high-speed applicability. Sub-harmonic self-
excitation [3] can provide brushless operation, but it requires
rotor harmonic windings and diode rectification and does not
naturally provide reversible field current. Unipolar OPT [6]
offers a simple high-speed rotor structure, but it is limited to
fixed-polarity excitation. Primary-side bipolar IPT [5] enables
stationary-side current reversal, but still requires WPT coils
and receiver-side circuitry. In contrast, the proposed system
occupies the previously unfilled intersection of bipolar ex-
citation, primary-side control, and zero rotor-side switching
components. Its main trade-off is the return-path voltage drop
and associated local loss in the conducting dark PV cell and
external diode.

VI. CONCLUSION
A pole-changing WFSM with bipolar OPT excitation has
been proposed and analyzed. The method enables field-current
reversal by using the first cell of the non-illuminated PV group
and an external diode as a passive return path, eliminating
rotor-side active switches, active converters, and resonant com-
pensation components. The complete excitation architecture
uses two optical channels for bipolar field groups and one
additional optical channel for unipolar field groups. A unified
I–V model was developed for the illuminated and dark PV
regimes, leading to a corrected MPP matching condition that
links the OPT parameters to the rotor field winding design.
FEA results confirmed the intended pole transformation, airgap
flux distributions, no-load EMF waveforms, and loss perfor-
mance in both pole configurations. After the final torque and
thermal results are inserted, the proposed topology can be
positioned as a brushless bipolar excitation solution for PC-
WFSMs, particularly in high-speed applications where rotor-
side complexity must be minimized.
