## Strongest kill argument (the reviewer comment)
> "The scheme is the dual-inverter sub-harmonic brushless WRSM of Ali et al. (TMAG 2015) and Rafin et al. (2023, 8-pole/48-slot), generalised to 16 inverters. Its only distinctive feature, selecting field polarity by the injected pattern, serves a pole-changing synchronous machine that the authors' own analysis shows has no benefit. As a plain exciter it matches WPT efficiency, but it gives up 1.5 points of start grade (12.0 to 10.5 %) or 13 % more inverter current, which is roughly 13 % of the 300 kW inverter's kVA spent to avoid a 4-12 kW exciter. The comparison is also unfair: brushes (about 97 % chain) and a commercial rotary transformer are not costed, and the benefit (no stationary exciter) is never given in money, mass or volume."

Supporting points:
1. **The start penalty depends on one unmade decision, and our model cannot settle it.** Peak-sum rule: grade 10.5 %. Rms-sum rule: 11.9 %. At start the 661 Hz injection and the near-DC torque current are incommensurate, so every coil reaches I_fund + I_inj in every cycle. Whether that peak is allowed depends on the GaN module's trip level and current-sensor range, not on its thermal rating. Until the SPB hardware's peak limit is stated, the value score swings between 2 (peak) and 3 (rms). The start penalty is also sensitive to the model: L_m(order 8) = 43 uH carries a 0.7 permeance factor and has no saturation, and the WRSM gap is open (2 mm gives 14.5 % I_max, 3 mm gives 17.8 %).
2. **New finding: the injection is not module-balanced at every speed (H10 link).** The analyst's balance statement ("not divisible by 3, so every module stays balanced") covers only the module current sum (KCL). It does not cover module power. Below 90 km/h the order-8 wave rotates backward (f_i = f_r0 - 8 f_m). On the module rule (coils j, j+16, j+32) its three coil currents then have the **same phase sequence as the 8-pole (order-4) torque wave**. When the two stator frequencies coincide, the cross power V_fund x I_inj has a nonzero average in each module. That happens at f_i = f_1, i.e. 12 f_m = f_r0, so f_m = 55.1 Hz, 3305 rpm, about **60 km/h**, a common long-haul and regional speed. The average is +/- 3 V_1,rms I_inj,rms cos(phi_0 + j pi/2): modules j = 0 mod 4 get +, j = 2 mod 4 get -, odd j get the sine term, and the sum over modules is 0. With V_1 about 28 V and I_inj 22-37 A (0.08-0.14 I_max), that is **about +/- 1.9-3.1 kW per module, 13-20 % of module power** at the 50-60 km/h load points. Near the coincidence speed it appears as a power swing at the beat |12 f_m - f_r0|. Below about 20 Hz beat (+/- 100 rpm, +/- 1.8 km/h around 60 km/h) this is hundreds of joules per module against about 1 J stored in a 75 V cell capacitor. A series stack cannot absorb it passively. Checks: forward injection on 2p = 8 is counter-sequence (no average); in 2p = 4 the backward wave is counter-sequence, and the forward wave coincides only at f_m = f_r0/6 (6611 rpm, above 90 km/h). PWM sidebands and the 5th/7th time harmonics of the torque current add further, weaker coincidence speeds. The screen did not model any of this.
3. **The baseline is not yet fair.** "No stationary hardware" is compared only with P1 WPT (link 94 % assumed). An industrial WRSM uses brushes or a rotary transformer. The rotor hardware (polyphase order-8 winding, tanks, rectifier, P1 switches) is about the same as a WPT receiver. The saving is therefore the stationary transmitter only, and the screen gives it no mass or cost.
4. **Dynamics and NVH are weaker than with a dedicated exciter.** 6.6 kW of forcing gives 1.65x field voltage. WRSM field reversal then takes 1.4 tau_f instead of 0.7 tau_f, and field-weakening transients (torque response) slow by the same factor. The order-4 force at 661 Hz during start is a tonal source on the softest stator mode of an 8-pole machine, and it occurs exactly when the cabin is quiet.

## What would save it
One decision plus one analysis, both cheap:
1. **Fix the current rule from the SPB GaN hardware.** If the module's peak limit (sensor range and trip) leaves headroom of at least I_fund,max + 0.12 I_max, i.e. if I_max in the envelope is a thermal rating, the start cost falls to 12.0 to 11.9 % and the -15 % envelope dip becomes about -9 % (rms rule). Value rises to 3 for the standalone exciter. If not, report the "+13 % current" as kVA cost against a rotary transformer or brushes in EUR and kg. That comparison decides H03s.
2. **Add the module power-balance rule to the screen.** The admissible injection must be counter-sequence to the torque wave within each module, or frequency-separated from it by more than the balancing bandwidth. Options at the 60 km/h coincidence: switch to forward injection in a speed band around 3305 rpm (f_s about 1100 Hz; check the voltage), or move f_r0 above 12 f_m,max (about 1 kHz; this costs P90, e.g. f_r0 = 1.5 kHz gives -12 %), or let H10 balancing absorb +/- 2-3 kW per module. If one of these closes the gap at under about 0.2 pt of efficiency, feasibility rises to 4.

With both positive, H03s scores about N2.5 V3 F4 E5 P4, i.e. 3.4-3.6. That is still below 4.0 because novelty is capped by Ali2015/Rafin2023/Talebi2022. It can reach promote level only inside H10, where the module-balance rule is the SPB-specific contribution.

## Should H03 combine with H10?
**Yes, as a test case, not as a merge.** H03 is the cleanest example of H10's claim: an injection that is current-balanced per module (KCL holds, no zero sequence) can still be **power-unbalanced** per module, here at a single, predictable speed with a j mod 4 pattern. H10 should take (a) the sequence rule (same sequence as the torque wave gives an average cross power at frequency coincidence; counter-sequence gives none), (b) the +/- 3 V_1 I_inj per-module bound and (c) the beat-frequency energy-swing check. H03s keeps the machine-side screen (start penalty, NVH, harvesting winding, baseline cost).

## Spin-off ideas
1. **H03s, the SPB as its own brushless exciter for a fixed-pole WRSM/HESM.** Drop pole changing. The claim to test: "on an SPB, an independent order not divisible by 3, made counter-sequence to the torque wave within every module, excites a fixed 8-pole WRSM at 0.1 pt efficiency cost and zero stationary hardware, with the start penalty set by the GaN peak-current rule." Baseline: brushes plus H-bridge, rotary transformer, P1 WPT, all costed. The HESM s = 0.25 row from H07 is the natural pairing: field need falls to 0.5 kW at cruise and 3.3 kW at start, so the start penalty and forcing gap shrink (forcing 6.6 kW already covers the 6 kW HESM need).
2. **Field current sensing without telemetry from the stator side.** The tuned harvesting tank reflects the rectifier load into the order-8 stator impedance. The SPB already drives that order, so it could estimate I_f, and possibly rotor temperature, from the order-8 voltage/current ratio. P1 has no signal across the gap either, so this answers a known WRSM gap (rotor field and temperature without slip rings or telemetry). Kill check first: the reflected-impedance sensitivity to I_f must exceed the L_m uncertainty from saturation.
