## Strongest kill argument (the reviewer comment)
> "The 'pulsation law' is the power invariance of the vector-space decomposition: in an m-phase set, cross power between two components exists only if they share a plane, i.e. m | (a ± b). For the authors' three-phase cells this says no more than that a positive- and a negative-sequence set (or two sets at different frequencies) produce oscillating instantaneous power, which is the textbook result behind 2f DC-link ripple under unbalance and behind MMC cell-capacitor ripple. The claimed impossibility ('a 3-phase SPB cannot do torque-gap-free pole changing') comes from worst-case steady-state products and an MLCC-only cell. With a crossfade envelope and a DC-link energy typical of a 300 kW inverter, the overlapped handover fits in three-phase cells. In any case a 0.13 s torque dip is shorter than a truck AMT gear shift. Changing the machine to five phases to avoid adding a few millifarads is not justified."

Quantitative backing, worked through:
1. **Triviality.** For m-leg cell k with leg angle 2 pi k/m: sum_k v_a,k i_b,k = (V I / 2) sum_k [cos((w_a+w_b)t - (a+b)theta_k) + cos((w_a-w_b)t - (a-b)theta_k)]. The geometric sums give m or 0. That is the whole derivation. For m = 3 every injectable order is +1 or -1 mod 3, so one of a ± b is always divisible by 3. This is what "isolated neutral removes zero sequence, leaves only +/- sequence" already implies. A reviewer will see it in five minutes. Novelty therefore has to come from consequences and design, not from the identity.
2. **The m = 3 shortfall is overstated by about 4x and depends on the capacitance assumption.**
   - Envelope: `transition_IM_handover` uses 3(V8 I4 + V4 I8) with full steady-state values = 8.46 kW. In a crossfade each product scales as x(1-x), with peak 0.25 at mid-handover. That gives about 2.1 kW, C_req about **5.7 mF** at +/-5 %, and **2.9 mF at +/-10 %**. The 100 V GaN on a 75 V cell has room for a 0.25 s +10 % excursion, which the screen's +/-5 % does not use. At the nominal 2.88 mF the overlap is then marginal, not impossible. The IM's flux decay is not linear, so the true factor is between 0.25 and 1. The simulator must give it.
   - Lean cell: 2.88 mF per cell over N = 16 is equivalent to C/N = 180 uF at 1200 V, about 130 J. A conventional 300 kW, 1200 V-class inverter with about 400 uF film stores about 290 J. Matching that energy per cell gives **about 6.4 mF per cell**, and that covers the corrected m = 3 overlap (5.7 mF). The "1.0-2.9 mF" carries the whole "cannot". The 0.35 retention is the worst case and nobody has measured it.
   - Cost of the fix: the pulsation is a 210 Hz transient (about 28 A at 75 V for 0.25 s), so a 100 V aluminium-polymer or hybrid electrolytic bulk is enough, not MLCC. Order of magnitude: 4-5 x 1 mF/100 V cans per cell, about 50 cm3 per cell, about 0.8 L and tens of EUR for the stack (my estimate; analyst to cost it). For the m = 5 alternative: with N = 16 kept (the 75 V class is fixed by Enes's EPC2361 loss model), legs go from 48 to 80, so gate drivers, isolated supplies and current sensors rise by 67 %. You also need an 80c-slot five-phase winding. Changing N instead (N = 12, 100 V cells) breaks the 100 V GaN choice. H04 found no torque/kVA gain from m = 5 and -0.08 pt cruise efficiency, and the balancing knob gets weaker (orders 9/14/19 for p = 4, low k_w; not even quantified for m = 5 in the screen). **On these numbers "m = 3 + bulk" wins the transition case.**
3. **"m = 5 gives zero" holds only in the linear model.** The two IM fluxes share the iron, so saturation creates intermodulation orders a ± b and 2a ± b at combination frequencies. For the 8/4 pair (a = 4, b = 2 in the screen's units):
   - The order a - b = 2 = b appears at frequency f_a - f_b ≠ f_b. A component that shares spatial order b but has a different frequency always cross-couples with the order-b current (order difference 0, divisible by any m). That gives cell pulsation for **every m**.
   - The order a + b = 6 EMF times the order-4 current gives 6 + 4 = 10, which is divisible by 5. That pulsates for m = 5 but not for m = 7.

   The residual is "saturation-intermodulation EMF x I". It is plausibly a few percent of 3VI, i.e. hundreds of W, which is the same order as the 374-1069 W allowance. Only a nonlinear model (the simulator's MEC, `spb_core.py`) can size it. Until then "m = 5/7 makes overlap admissible" is an unproven claim for exactly the case that headlines the paper.
4. **The transition benefit is small for a truck.**
   - From `im_pole_transition.json`: the best overlapped schedule (t_d = 0.25 s) still has min 80 % torque and 56 ms below 90 %. The near-sequential schedule (t_d = 0.01 s) has 134 ms below 90 % with a -116 Nm reversal. Speed drop is 0.002 km/h on a flat road.
   - On a 2 % grade the gap is 0.5 s however much overlap is used, because it is voltage-limited and m does not change that.
   - Heavy-truck AMT shifts interrupt torque for several hundred ms (lit-scout to cite a number), and Libbos2025bumpless reports a 200 ms flux transition.

   What overlap actually buys is avoiding the -116 Nm reversal, which is a driveline-clunk issue. A ramped sequential schedule (torque down, demagnetise, rebuild, like a gear shift) can probably remove that without overlap.
5. **A gap in the screen that cuts both ways.** A "sequential" m = 3 handover is not pulsation-free either. While the residual 8-pole rotor flux (tau_r about 0.5 s) still induces V8 at the terminals, the term 3 V8 I4 is present even with I8 = 0. To stay under the 374-1069 W allowance with I4 = 86 A, V8 must be below about 1.5-4.1 V, i.e. 6-18 % of 22.3 V. Passive decay to that level takes about 1.4 s. Active demagnetisation must finish before the order-4 current rises. If this holds, it **strengthens** H11: an m = 3 SPB cannot change IM poles quickly even sequentially without bulk capacitance. But nobody has computed it. Note also that the 0.134 s figure comes from a schedule with a 10 ms overlap.

## What would save it (the one analysis that raises value, the weakest score)
**Move the value case from the transition to steady-state multi-order operation, and put a cost on "m vs capacitance".** In order:
1. **confirm_H10 with the nonlinear MEC** (simulator): cell power and cell-capacitor voltage over the real IM schedules (overlapped and sequential, flat and 2 % grade) for m = 3 and m = 5. Include saturation intermodulation and the residual-flux V8 x I4 term. Report the true envelope factor (0.25-1) and the m = 5 residual. That alone sets feasibility to 4 or kills the m = 5 claim.
2. **One cost table** (analyst): for each use case, m = 3 + bulk C (mF, cm3, EUR, ESR loss, lifetime at 105 °C) vs m = 5 (legs, drivers, isolated supplies, winding, balancing headroom in W/A). Use cases: (a) IM handover, a transient; (b) **H03s continuous harmonic excitation**, where m = 3 needs 5-7 mF at a *continuous* 56 A, 350-480 Hz ripple, so bulk electrolytics heat up and lose lifetime, and m = 5 with counter-sequence orders is a clean cure; (c) post-bypass operation with N - 1 cells, where the rule's c = Q/(mN) stops being an integer and unequal coil loading appears. That table is still to compute. If m = 5 wins (b) or (c) by a clear margin, value reaches 3-4.
3. **A low-power experiment** on the Rev B cell: a 3-leg cell into an RL load with two current sets (orders 4 and 2 mapped to legs, f_a = 140 Hz, f_b = 70 Hz). Measure the capacitor ripple against 3 V I / (2 pi (f_a + f_b) V C). Then use a 5-leg cell (two boards) to show the cancellation. That turns the identity into a verified design law, at almost no cost.

Path to >= 4.0: N4 V4 F4 E4 P4. N4 needs the generalised design law (phase-number selection for any multi-order operation, with the saturation residual bounded and the capacitance-energy equivalence to a common bus) plus a full-text check that Zoric2018arbitrary, Subotic2019active and Sau2019modular do not already state it. V4 needs item 2 showing a steady-state case where m = 5 wins. F4 needs items 1 and 3. Without a steady-state win, the ceiling is about 3.4-3.6.

## Strongest paper framing and venue
Do not sell "3-phase SPB cannot do X". Sell **a design law with two positive designs**:
- (i) The law and the per-cell capacitance it requires: C_min = m V_a I_b k_env / (2 pi f_ab V_cell dV). Include the energy equivalence to a common-bus link, which shows why common-bus PPM drives (Libbos, Wu/Peretti) never saw it.
- (ii) Design 1: an m = 3 SPB with a pulsation-constrained transition schedule and minimum bulk C.
- (iii) Design 2: an m = 5 SPB where overlapped pole change and exciterless harmonic excitation are admissible at the existing 75 V MLCC cell.
- (iv) A cost crossover between the two designs.
- (v) The low-power experiment.

Pole changing is the motivating case, not the title. Harmonic excitation, series-connected multi-machine operation and post-fault operation are also covered by the same law, which widens the audience beyond PPM.

Venue: **IEEE Transactions on Power Electronics, Letters**, which fits a law, a sizing rule and an experiment. Alternatively **ECCE 2027** first, then a full **IEEE TIE** paper once the m = 5 design and the cost crossover exist. TTE only if the truck use case (H02r/H03s) carries value.

## Spin-off ideas
1. **H11s, pulsation-constrained transition scheduling for m = 3 SPB.** The claim to test: an IM 8->4 handover can be scheduled (demagnetise -> rebuild with a bounded cross product) to keep each cell inside +/-10 % at the existing 2.9 mF, with a torque gap of <= 0.2 s and no torque reversal. This is a positive, zero-hardware result on Enes's existing hardware. It is a constrained version of the 27-schedule search already in `run_im_transition_opt.py`.
2. **Module interleaving as a fixed-pole SPB rule.** The screen found that block (non-interleaved) module partitions under 10 % static eccentricity give an 18.5 % average cell imbalance (16-46 kV/s drift), while interleaved partitions give about 0. That applies to every SPB machine, including Ghent's modular axial-flux one, which uses block modules. Check against Verkroost2024PowerDistribution before claiming it.
