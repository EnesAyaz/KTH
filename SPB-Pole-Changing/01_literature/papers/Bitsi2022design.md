# Bitsi2022design

**Design Aspects and Performance Evaluation of Pole-Phase Changing Induction Machines** (2022), Energies. DOI: https://doi.org/10.3390/en15197012

Access: abstract + full text read.

- **Claim:** Pole-phase changing extends the torque-speed envelope and raises efficiency over a large part of the operating region of WICSC IMs; benefit depends on the original pole number.
- **Method:** FEM of two 20 kW, 64 N m, 3000 rpm IMs (original 2-pole and 6-pole), max-torque-per-ampere and max-efficiency maps.
- **Machine / inverter:** WICSC IM; DC bus 300 V (machine limit 150 V), current limit 22 A (about 2.5x rated torque).
- **Power / voltage / speed numbers:** 2-pole design: best pole number changes 2 to 4 at about 2x rated torque; 4-pole reaches the voltage limit near 2400 rpm and 2-pole is best again at 2645 rpm; with current limit 18 A or less pole changing does not extend the envelope; 4-pole has lowest losses above 73 N m at 1500 rpm (less rotor copper, more stator iron). 6-pole design: 8-pole gives up to 12 % more torque below 1680 rpm; 2- and 4-pole widen flux weakening; sequence 2-4-2 between 3880 and 6000 rpm.
- **Relevance to SPB:** Quantifies when pole changing helps; benefit is mostly efficiency and depends on the voltage/current limits that SPB sets.
- **Limitations:** FEM only, 20 kW, no drive cycle, no truck, no inverter loss.
