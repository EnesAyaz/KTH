# Libbos2022inverter

**Inverter Design Considerations for Variable-Pole Induction Machines in Electric Vehicles** (2022), IEEE Transactions on Power Electronics. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1109/tpel.2022.3177082

Access: abstract record read (OpenAlex/Crossref); full text not read (IEEE Xplore returns an anti-bot page, HTTP 418, to curl even through the VPN; stopped for that host).

- **Claim:** Inverter design framework for a variable-pole IM: an 18-leg drive reconfiguring a six-pole IM to four- and two-pole raises torque capability at maximum speed by 2.2x over a 3-leg fixed-pole design; a 3-leg drive would need 1.5x oversizing.
- **Method:** Generalised design framework, loss minimisation with pole count as a degree of freedom, 18-leg GaN 890 VA experimental inverter with toroidally wound IM.
- **Machine / inverter:** 18-leg GaN inverter, toroidal IM, 6/4/2-pole.
- **Power / voltage / speed numbers:** 18-leg drive: 50 % lower losses and 62 % less dc-link capacitance than 3-leg; variable-pole operation cuts combined machine plus drive losses by up to 45 % at partial load and high speed.
- **Relevance to SPB:** Closest published analogue to SPB: many low-voltage-device legs, pole count as an efficiency degree of freedom, GaN. Gives IM partial-load loss reduction evidence (the IM has large partial-load losses that pole changing removes).
- **Limitations:** 890 VA lab inverter; partial-load, not truck cycle.
