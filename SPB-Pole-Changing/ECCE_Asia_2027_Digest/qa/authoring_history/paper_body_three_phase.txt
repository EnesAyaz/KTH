p('Abstract - A stacked polyphase bridge can reuse independently controlled three-phase winding groups for pole changing while distributing a high traction DC voltage among low-voltage converter cells. We investigate the joint choice of cell count, stator winding and pole ratio for hybrid-excited synchronous machines. All groups are three wire, with no neutral conductor. A constructive winding screen covers higher pole counts, including a 12/6-pole example with 24 cells and 72 coil positions. Its two pitch factors are both 0.866. A linear desired-order dq model compares a pole-changing hybrid machine against a matched 12-pole IPMSM reference and a stronger-field-weakening IPMSM sensitivity. In the illustrative case, selecting six poles extends the 150 kW electrical envelope from 4450 rpm to the 9000 rpm study ceiling, while the 300 kW range is unchanged. The result is conditional on rotor flux, back-iron capacity and modal inductances; it is not a general superiority claim.','Abstract',11)
h('Introduction')
p('Megawatt charging for heavy-duty electric vehicles motivates interest in high-voltage vehicle architectures [1]. For a given power, a higher DC voltage reduces current. Conventional two-level traction inverters expose each switch to the full DC-link voltage plus transients, which constrains device selection, switching energy, cooling and electromagnetic compatibility. The feasible switching frequency also affects current ripple, machine harmonic loss and common-mode excitation.')
p('Multilevel converters, including neutral-point-clamped and flying-capacitor implementations, can reduce device voltage stress and output-voltage steps. Their common-mode and loss benefits depend on the topology and modulation [2]. Additional balancing, control and protection are required; component count alone is insufficient to rank reliability. Likewise, fault tolerance requires explicit detection, isolation and reconfiguration provisions.')
p('The stacked polyphase bridge (SPB) distributes the link voltage among series DC cells. Each cell supplies a separate three-phase, three-wire winding group, with no neutral connection between cells or to the DC link. This structure permits lower-voltage devices, including suitable GaN devices, and independent group-current commands. It can therefore supply the stator rephasing needed for pole changing without stator contactors, provided the winding and rotor are compatible.')
p('The research question is which combinations of stacked-cell count and pole-changing ratio are usable. A 4/2-pole example is not sufficient for higher-pole traction machines: absolute pole count, slot layout and rotor geometry must also be specified. This digest develops an explicit 12/6-pole winding and compares hybrid-excited and IPMSM torque and power envelopes. Phase count is held at three throughout; other phase counts are reserved for later work.')

sub('A constructive family for integer pole ratios')
p('Let PH and PL be total poles, pH = PH/2 and pL = PL/2. In the selected family, the three phase-coil axes are spaced by 120°/pL mechanically. The low-pole phase displacement is 120°; the high-pole displacement is r times this value, where r = pH/pL. Thus an integer ratio r not divisible by three preserves balanced three-phase operation. A 2:1 change reverses phase sequence; the commands are adjusted to preserve mechanical rotation direction. A ratio divisible by three fails this construction, without proving that every alternative winding is impossible.')

h('Conclusion')
p('The proposed contribution is a co-design map from cell voltage and cell count to a usable winding and pole ratio, followed by rotor and speed-envelope checks. A concrete 24-cell, 72-coil 12/6-pole candidate demonstrates that three-phase groups without a neutral lead can support higher-pole traction concepts. In the illustrative hybrid case, pole changing extends the reduced-power electrical range while leaving the full-power range unchanged. The outcome supports further winding and rotor optimization; it does not yet select a universally superior machine or an optimal cell count.')
