Joint Selection of GaN SPB Cells and Pole-Changing
Windings for Truck Propulsion

Abstract—Stacked polyphase bridges (SPBs) enable low-voltage power devices to supply high-voltage truck propulsion systems while independently controlling multiple stator winding groups. This paper presents a device–winding co-design method that exploits these groups for electronic pole changing. Discrete GaN voltage ratings, cell-voltage stress, coil allocation, three-phase balance, and pole ratio are treated as coupled constraints. A constructive winding analysis compares 2:1, 3:1, and 5:2 pole ratios. For a 1.2-kV link, the investigated design space includes a 150-V, 16-cell, 48-slot 8/4-pole configuration and a 200-V, 12-cell, 36-slot 12/6-pole configuration. An explicit 36-slot construction gives a winding factor of 0.866 in both 12- and 6-pole modes while cancelling the alternate selected stator order. The resulting candidates establish selected-order winding and voltage compatibility within the investigated family. An illustrative dq sensitivity study addresses possible speed-envelope extension; physical-machine performance remains to be validated.

Index Terms—Electric truck propulsion, gallium nitride, hybrid excitation, pole changing, stacked polyphase bridge.

Introduction

Megawatt-level charging of battery-electric trucks motivates higher battery and dc-link voltages to limit current and cable loss at a given power [1]. In a conventional two-level truck propulsion inverter, each switch must block the full dc-link voltage together with switching overshoot. Increasing the blocking-voltage requirement introduces conduction- and switching-loss trade-offs that constrain the simultaneous improvement of efficiency and switching frequency. Limited switching frequency increases current-ripple-related machine losses, while common-mode voltage (CMV), high dv/dt, and associated insulation and bearing stress must also be addressed.

Multilevel inverters provide an alternative to full-link-voltage switching. Three-, five-, and seven-level neutral-point-clamped (NPC) or flying-capacitor (FC) implementations can employ lower-blocking-voltage devices and generate smaller output-voltage steps. With appropriate modulation, these steps can reduce current distortion and CMV [2]. However, the additional switches, clamping or flying-capacitor networks, and balancing functions introduce further failure mechanisms and implementation complexity. Fault-tolerant operation requires explicit redundancy, fault isolation, or reconfiguration; it does not follow inherently from the number of voltage levels.

The SPB offers a modular route to voltage partitioning: series-connected dc cells supply separate polyphase winding groups [2]. Low cell voltages permit suitable low-voltage GaN devices, while the machine is implemented with multiple three-phase winding sets whose neutral points remain isolated. The resulting independently controlled inverter units are already required by the voltage architecture. Their current phase and sequence can therefore be used as additional machine-control degrees of freedom.

Pole changing can exploit this flexibility to trade low-speed torque against high-speed capability and operating-point losses. Variable-pole induction drives have demonstrated partial-load and high-speed loss benefits [3], and pole-changing electric-vehicle drives have been investigated for wide-speed propulsion requirements [4]. Such benefits motivate drive-cycle-dependent pole selection, although net cycle savings must include converter and excitation losses. Conventional implementations use Dahlander-type stator reconnection or multiple inverter units to reverse selected coil-group currents. Purpose-designed synchronous and hybrid-excited rotors can change their field polarity accordingly [5]. An SPB can reuse its inverter units for this stator rephasing without adding dedicated stator converters, provided the winding is admissible in both modes.

Prior SPB voltage partitioning [2] and electronic pole changing [3]–[5] do not alone determine compatible cell and winding counts. This paper adds a construction-specific selection method: balanced isolated three-phase groups and alternate-order stator-MMF cancellation are checked jointly with discrete device ratings and coil allocation. The analytical result is a reproducible admissibility frontier, rather than a manufactured machine or an efficiency optimum.

Balanced current commands are insufficient by themselves: the phase back-EMF magnitudes, phase displacement, and impedance must also be compatible with balanced operation at both selected pole counts. Equal modal gain and equal current amplitudes give equal ideal steady-state cell power for a single selected sinusoidal field. This does not establish balance under additional rotor harmonics, regeneration, or transitions; capacitor sizing and active balance control remain design tasks.

For Q conventional double-layer coil positions, the assumed one-coil-per-position construction gives C = Q independently connected coils. At least one coil per phase branch requires 3N ≤ C; equal allocation additionally requires integer c = C/(3N). A conventional single-layer arrangement instead gives C = Q/2. Additional independently fed conductor subdivisions can change these bounds, but require separate lead, insulation, and manufacturing analysis. The slot bounds used here are therefore construction specific.

Device voltage sets a lower bound on N, whereas winding allocation limits the admissible values. Neither criterion alone determines the final cell count. The number of switch positions is 6N before device paralleling, so increasing N also increases gate-drive and interconnection requirements.

Winding Admissibility for Pole Changing

In the investigated family, the three phase-coil axes are displaced by 120°/g mechanically. Their electrical phase displacements are therefore 120a and 120b degrees. Equation (3) is a phase-axis and allocation screen, not a sufficient machine-feasibility theorem. Primitive triples are ordered by slot offset and then repeated sector; consecutive blocks of c = Q/(3N) triples form each cell. Equal modal magnitudes across phases and cells and alternate-order rejection are checked to 10⁻⁸ relative tolerance. Current commands align with the selected-order coil phasors. Balanced impedance, mutual coupling, and rotor-harmonic back-EMF are not established by this check.

A. Pole ratio and coil span

The reduced orders of a 2:1 combination are 2 and 1, yielding opposite phase sequences. The 5:2 combination also satisfies (3), with equal sequences. A 3:1 combination gives a zero-sequence spatial order within this uniform-triple family and is excluded by the three-wire connection. This result applies to the stated construction; it does not exclude alternative distributed, reconfigurable, or nonuniform winding families.

B. Reproducible 12 Cell Winding

For Q = 36, N = 12, and coil pitch y = 4, define q = 0, …, 3, t = 0, …, 2, and phase index k = 0, 1, 2. Cell j = 3q + t + 1 has start slot s = 1 + q + 12t + 4k and return slot 1 + [(s + 3) mod 36], with identical coil polarity. Relative current phases are 60q + 240k degrees for 12 poles and 30q + 120k degrees for 6 poles; a common torque-angle offset is allowed. Table II checks this construction.

With θs = 2π(s − 1)/36, define Gν = Σs Is[exp(−iνθs) − exp(−iνθreturn)]/72 and Is = exp(ipθs). Thus |Gp| is the winding factor and max(|Gother|, |G−other|)/|Gp| is the alternate-order residual. The same grouping procedure gives a two-cell 36-slot example with factors 0.750/0.837; its one-cell counterpart fails alternate-order rejection. This is construction specific, not a universal cell-count minimum.

Cell and Winding Selection Results

The search uses N = 1–32, Q = 18, 24, …, 96, and pole counts H/L = 8/4, 12/6, 16/8, 24/12, 32/16, 12/4, 24/8, 10/4, and 20/8. Integer coil pitches 1–Q/2 maximize the smaller winding factor. For each rating and pole pair, accepted candidates are ranked by lowest N, then Q. Passenger-car examples (48 slots/8 poles [7], [8]; 72/12 [9]) motivate a sensitivity range, not truck design limits. Q ≤ 60 and Q ≤ 72 are study constraints.

A. Planned Building Block and Machine Design

The full paper will design a three-phase building block using 200-V GaN devices for the 12-cell candidate, addressing paralleling, gate driving, dc capacitance, voltage balance, and electrothermal losses. The corresponding 36-slot 12/6-pole hybrid-excited machine will be designed using finite-element analysis (FEA), evaluating saturation, back-EMF, torque ripple, and modal inductances in both modes.

Conclusion

The constructed 12-cell/36-slot winding supports selected 12/6-pole stator orders with isolated three-phase groups. Joint voltage and winding constraints determine cell count; this does not yet establish an optimized truck propulsion machine.

The full paper will replace the illustrative parameters with FEA-derived flux maps and compare loss-aware envelopes and representative truck drive-cycle energy against an equal-volume IPMSM, including excitation and transition constraints.
